## Prioritized corrections

### P1 — restore standard forms with definitions and one worked conversion

The pre-rewrite text explicitly covered reversing a `>=` row, expressing a free variable as a difference, and converting inequalities to equalities with slack variables. Those topics are now absent; line 179 nevertheless uses “fri dualvariabel” and “fri primalvariabel” without explaining “fri”. An `A_eq` reference in the final API table does not teach these transformations.

Add a compact depth passage beside the model/solver transition:

- Define a free variable as one allowed to take any real value. Show `t=t_+-t_-`, with both new variables nonnegative.
- Show `t>=2` becoming `-t<=-2`; this directly explains the second row of the impossible-model code already on the page.
- Define a slack variable through `2x+y+s_1=9`, `s_1>=0`; at `(4,1)`, `s_1=0`, while the labor equation has `s_2=3`.
- Explain that equivalent forms describe the same feasible choices, and that the solver accepts equality rows through `A_eq`, `b_eq`.

Keep this optional and concrete. No simplex tableau is needed. “Affin” and “relaksasjon” are already taught in the approved Week 8; their omission as repeated labels is not itself a blocking loss. “Polyeder” could be restored as a short definition if it remains required source vocabulary.

### P1 — make complementary slackness a worked tool

Lines 173–179 correctly state the gap identity and the two complementary-slackness rules, but the former worked derivation of the prices has disappeared. Restore it immediately after the numerical slack vector:

`4-x=1>0` forces `w=0`; positive production of both products forces `2u+v+w=5` and `u+2v=4`; solving gives `u=2`, `v=1`. Verify nonnegativity and the product-price inequalities afterward. This explains where the apparently chosen prices came from without reversing the successful “numerical proof first” teaching order.

Explicitly connect this to optimality: for a feasible plan and feasible prices, all five products being zero is equivalent to a zero gap and hence certifies both optima. An active constraint need not have positive price; do not imply the converse.

Strengthen the written part of existing math Oppgave 2 so students use these facts to recover or justify the prices. Keep the six-widget count and its existing exact answer fields.

### P2 — finish the changed-capacity guarantee with new prices

Line 189 correctly asserts that `(4,5/2)` gives the new optimum 30 at `Delta=2`, but the displayed old certificate only proves the weaker upper bound 31. Add the new certificate:

`2(x+2y)+3x = 5x+4y <= 2*9+3*4 = 30`.

The new prices are `(u,v,w)=(0,2,3)` and the plan attains the bound. This short calculation both proves the claim and explains the changed resource values: the machine constraint has slack `1/2`, and labor plus the A-limit determine the answer. At `Delta=3/2`, these same optimal prices also give a concrete example of an active machine constraint with zero price.

Replace the layout description “De to loddrett plasserte figurene” with an explanation of what the upper/lower panels show.

### P2 — retain worked examples for the remaining asserted concepts

The original explicit multiple-optima example was removed, and simplex is now only named (line 90). Two brief optional examples suffice:

- With objective `2x+y` on the same production polygon, both `(4,1)` and `(3,3)`, and every point on their connecting edge, give the maximum 9. This illustrates “an optimum exists at a corner” versus “all optima must be corners”.
- Describe simplex at the intended outline level as moving between adjacent corners without decreasing the objective. The path `(0,0) -> (4,0) -> (4,1) -> (3,3)` gives values `0,20,24,27` on this polygon. Do not present it as a guarantee that every implementation takes that path.

For the corner proof itself, a midpoint calculation such as `(3.5,2)=((4,1)+(3,3))/2`, giving `P=25.5=(24+27)/2`, would make “veid gjennomsnitt” concrete. State that the weights are nonnegative and sum to one. Existing Oppgave 1 can include a short written comparison of corners to assess goal 2 without adding another widget.
