## Mathematical audit

### Cholesky and its connection to Newton/CG

The hypothesis is explicitly **symmetric positive definite**, not merely invertible or symmetric. With positive diagonal entries, `H = LL^T` is followed by the correct order `Ly = -g`, then `L^T p = y`. The helper vector is explicitly distinguished from the optimization point. The text correctly states that `np.linalg.cholesky` supplies the factor, not the Newton direction by itself.

For the worked example,

- `H = [[4,2],[2,3]]` is SPD (leading principal minors 4 and 8).
- `L = [[2,0],[1,sqrt(2)]]` gives `LL^T = H`.
- For `-g = (6,5)`, forward substitution gives `y = (3,sqrt(2))`.
- Back substitution gives `p = (1,1)` and `Hp = (6,5)`.

The three new exercises use an SPD matrix with leading principal minors 9 and 36. Their exact answers are:

1. Exercise 7: `(a,b,c) = (3,1,2)`.
2. Exercise 8: `(y1,y2) = (3,2)`.
3. Exercise 9: `(p1,p2) = (2/3,1)`.

All three were checked by exact rational matrix multiplication: `LL^T = H`, `Ly = (9,7)`, `L^T p = (3,2)`, and `Hp = (9,7)`. The fields request individual scalar answers, so no unsupported matrix-input contract is introduced. Week 6's forward reference uses the corresponding `Ly=b`, `L^T x=y` convention correctly and points to the new `uke10-cholesky` anchor.

### Assumptions and guarantees preserved across the package

- Interior stationarity remains necessary, not sufficient. The role of boundaries and isolated integer choices is preserved.
- A semidefinite Hessian with a zero eigenvalue is not treated as a decisive local classification. SPD at an exact stationary point gives a strict local minimum; global convexity tests apply across the domain.
- Existence uses continuity and a nonempty compact feasible set. Strict convexity gives at most one minimum, not existence by itself.
- Finite samples, a small gradient, solver success and a small step are consistently separated from a global proof.
- Armijo's termination argument requires a descent direction. A descent direction is not a guarantee for a full step, and a finite halving budget may fail.
- Newton's linear system is uniquely solvable under invertibility; SPD additionally makes the model a convex bowl and gives descent for nonzero gradient. Standard CG is restricted to SPD systems. The local quadratic-convergence statement retains its nearby-start, invertibility and Hessian-regularity assumptions.
- The LP dual uses nonnegative prices and `A^T q >= p` for the stated maximizing primal. Price bounds, complementary slackness, sign reversal of SciPy marginals and the sensitivity interval `[-9/2,3/2]` are correct. An active constraint is not claimed to require a positive price.
- Equality-constrained stationarity requires regularity. The plus-sign convention `L=f+lambda h` is consistent throughout week 12. The tangent-space second-order test uses the Hessian of `L`, not just the Hessian of `f`.
- Degeneracy correctly illustrates an optimum missed by the regular multiplier equations. The Lagrangian lower bound minimizes globally over all `z`, and positive curvature is used to justify the global minimum. No general strong-duality claim is made for arbitrary nonlinear equality problems.

All existing exact mathematical exercise answers in weeks 8, 9, 11 and 12 were reviewed and are consistent with their questions.
